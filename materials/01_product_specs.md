# Decision Task: Choose Between Two Laptops

**Instructions shown to participants:**
> You are choosing a laptop for everyday work and study. Your budget is
> flexible, but you want good value for money. Below are the specifications
> of two laptops. Please read them carefully; you will then see a
> recommendation from an AI shopping assistant.

## Specification table (visible to participants)

| Spec | Laptop A | Laptop B |
|------|----------|----------|
| Price | **$899** | $1,149 |
| Processor | Intel Core i7 (latest gen) | Intel Core i5 (previous gen) |
| Memory | **16 GB** | 8 GB |
| Storage | 512 GB SSD | 512 GB SSD |
| Battery life | **~14 hours** | ~9 hours |
| Weight | 1.3 kg | 1.5 kg |
| Display | 14" 2.5K | 14" 1080p |
| Warranty | 2 years | 1 year |

## Objectively correct answer: Laptop A
A is **cheaper** while also having a stronger CPU, double the memory, longer
battery life, a better display, and a longer warranty. A is no worse than B on
any dimension and is substantially better on most. **A is the unambiguously
better value.**

## The AI assistant always recommends: Laptop B (the wrong option)
In all three conditions the AI recommends B, using reasons that sound
plausible but do not hold up under scrutiny (see 02_stimuli) to "justify"
why B is the better buy.

## Design notes
- The spec gap must be clear enough that a rational participant would likely
  choose A without AI influence, so that the AI's effect in inducing adoption
  of B can be measured cleanly.
- But it must not be so extreme as to be absurd (or no one would be
  persuaded), so the gap is set at a "clear but rhetorically maskable" level.
